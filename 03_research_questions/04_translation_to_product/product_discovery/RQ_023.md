# RQ-023

## Question

**How did the completed neuroscience research reshape the product vision, and what strategic decisions were made before beginning product validation?**

---

## Status

**Answered**

---

**Product Discovery**

Following the completion of the neuroscience research phase, the project transitioned from answering scientific questions to identifying a commercially viable application of Targeted Memory Reactivation (TMR). The objective was no longer to determine whether TMR works, but to understand how scientifically validated TMR could create meaningful value in a real-world product.

The completed literature review established several important constraints that directly influenced the product vision. Experimental studies consistently demonstrated that TMR produces measurable improvements in memory consolidation under carefully controlled laboratory conditions, with moderate effect sizes and strict experimental requirements. Successful implementation depended on accurate sleep-stage detection, precise cue timing, reliable physiological monitoring, and controlled sensory stimulation. These findings indicated that any commercial implementation must preserve these biological requirements if it aims to reproduce the reported scientific outcomes.

The initial product concept considered developing a dedicated consumer hardware device capable of performing closed-loop TMR. However, further strategic analysis revealed significant limitations associated with this approach. The consumer wearable market is highly competitive, hardware development requires substantial financial investment and long development cycles, and previous companies with scientifically validated sleep technologies demonstrated that technical success alone does not guarantee commercial success.

As a result, the product vision was fundamentally revised. Rather than developing proprietary hardware, the project shifted toward a software-first strategy. The current working hypothesis is to develop a software layer that integrates with existing wearable devices capable of providing the physiological signals required for closed-loop TMR. This approach enables the project to leverage mature sensing hardware while concentrating development efforts on the core intellectual property: real-time physiological signal processing, sleep-stage detection, closed-loop decision making, and personalized cue delivery.

Several strategic decisions were established before beginning product validation:

1.The project will focus on software rather than proprietary hardware development.
2.The system will be designed as a real-time closed-loop architecture, allowing physiological signals to determine cue delivery dynamically instead of relying on predefined schedules.
3.Existing wearable ecosystems will be considered as potential integration platforms rather than direct competitors.
4.Product validation will precede system implementation, ensuring that technical feasibility, customer value, and market opportunity are validated before significant engineering resources are committed.
5.The remaining product decisions will be driven by evidence rather than assumptions, following the same research-first methodology used throughout the neuroscience phase.

At the conclusion of the product discovery phase, one central product hypothesis emerged:

**A software-based closed-loop TMR platform integrated with existing wearable hardware may provide a practical and scalable pathway for translating validated neuroscience into a commercial product.**

This hypothesis now serves as the foundation for the subsequent product validation phase, where its technical feasibility, user value, and commercial potential will be systematically investigated.
