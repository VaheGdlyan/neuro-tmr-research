# RQ-024

## Question

**Can PPG-based wearables provide sufficient real-time sleep-stage detection accuracy for closed-loop TMR?**

## Status

**Answered**

---

**Reason**

This determines whether our product can work on consumer wearables or requires EEG hardware. 


## Conclusion

After reviewing more than 20 scientific publications, experimental studies, and technical documentation related to PPG-based sleep monitoring, sleep-stage classification, and wearable-based physiological signal processing, it was found that PPG-based wearables demonstrate promising capability for estimating sleep stages in real-world conditions.

Modern wearable devices can use PPG-derived signals, including heart rate variability (HRV), pulse waveform characteristics, and autonomic nervous system indicators, combined with machine learning approaches, to estimate sleep stages. However, unlike EEG, PPG does not directly measure brain activity. Therefore, sleep-stage estimation from PPG represents an indirect inference process rather than a direct measurement of the physiological mechanisms underlying sleep stages.

The critical distinction for Neuro_TMR is that sleep-stage classification alone does not prove the feasibility of a closed-loop TMR system. Although PPG-based wearables may provide sufficient information for estimating sleep stages, there is currently limited evidence demonstrating that a fully PPG-based, stage-aware closed-loop TMR system can reliably detect memory-relevant sleep periods, deliver cues at the optimal moment, and produce measurable memory enhancement outcomes.

The existing scientific evidence supporting TMR is primarily based on EEG-confirmed sleep stages, particularly during NREM and slow-wave sleep (SWS), where brain activity patterns directly indicate the optimal window for memory reactivation. Therefore, replacing EEG with PPG introduces additional uncertainty in the most critical component of the system: precise timing of cue delivery.

Based on the current evidence, PPG-based wearables should not be considered the primary sensing technology for the first generation of Neuro_TMR. Instead, the initial product development will focus on an EEG-based stage-aware closed-loop TMR architecture because EEG provides direct measurement of brain activity and is the established method used in TMR research.

Future investigation of PPG-based implementations remains valuable. Once the core Neuro_TMR mechanism has been validated using EEG, PPG-based approaches can be explored as a second-generation solution focused on improving comfort, accessibility, and scalability.

## Product Decision

**Neuro_TMR V1 will use an EEG-based stage-aware closed-loop architecture.**

PPG-based sleep-stage detection remains a potential future direction but is currently considered insufficiently validated for the first product iteration due to the absence of direct evidence demonstrating successful PPG-based closed-loop TMR performance.
