## Why This Expert Matters

Scott Cairney is particularly relevant because his research connects TMR with emotional memory, memory strength, sleep spindles, and adaptive forgetting.

He can challenge the idea that TMR is simply a technique for strengthening memories and can help evaluate which memories are actually suitable for intervention.

## Relevant Research

### Foundational Work
- **Cairney et al. (2014) — "Targeted Memory Reactivation During Slow Wave Sleep Facilitates Emotional Memory Consolidation"**
  - Early TMR evidence linking cueing, emotional memory, and spindle-mediated processing.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.5665/sleep.3572

- **Cairney et al. (2016) — "The Benefits of Targeted Memory Reactivation for Consolidation in Sleep Are Contingent on Memory Accuracy and Direct Cue-Memory Associations"**
  - Shows that TMR outcome depends on prior memory quality and direct cue association.

- **Cairney et al. (2018) — "Memory Consolidation Is Linked to Spindle-Mediated Information Processing during Sleep"**
  - Relevant to whether sleep spindles represent an important intervention window.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2018.01.087

### Recent Work
- **Denis & Cairney (2023) — "Neural Reactivation During Human Sleep"**
  - Reviews evidence that memory reactivation is closely linked to slow-oscillation/spindle physiology.

- **Cairney (2024) — "Forgetting Unwanted Memories in Sleep"**
  - Develops the idea that sleep reactivation may support adaptive forgetting as well as consolidation.
  - Particularly relevant to assumptions about what TMR should be used to achieve.

## Relevance to Neuro-TMR

Cairney can challenge whether:
- all memories benefit equally from TMR;
- memory enhancement is always the desired outcome;
- encoding quality should be part of system eligibility;
- stage-aware cueing ignores important spindle-level physiology.

## Phase-I Claims They Can Challenge
- TMR effects can be generalized across adequately encoded memories.
- Cue association is a relatively straightforward prerequisite.
- TMR should mainly be framed as memory strengthening.
- Broad stage detection captures the most relevant physiology.

## Questions for This Expert
1. Your work shows that TMR benefits depend on pre-sleep memory accuracy and direct cue-memory associations. Should a practical system measure memory strength before deciding what to cue?
2. Given your work on spindle-mediated reactivation, is broad NREM/N3 detection sufficient for first-generation cue scheduling?
3. Your recent work emphasizes adaptive forgetting of unwanted memories. Does this change how we should think about the intended outcome and safety boundaries of TMR technology?
