## Why This Expert Matters

Bernhard Staresina is particularly important for Neuro-TMR because his research directly addresses the temporal organization of memory processing during sleep.

His work examines how slow oscillations, sleep spindles, and hippocampal ripples interact during memory consolidation, and his group has directly tested closed-loop TMR in which memory cues were synchronized to specific phases of ongoing slow oscillations.

This makes him one of the strongest experts for challenging a central Phase-I simplification:

> Can a first-generation TMR system reasonably operate at the level of sleep-stage detection, or does effective intervention require more precise event- or phase-level targeting?

He is therefore valuable not simply as a sleep-memory expert, but as someone whose empirical work can directly challenge the proposed stage-aware first-generation pathway.

## Relevant Research

### Foundational Work

- **Staresina et al. (2015) — "Hierarchical nesting of slow oscillations, spindles and ripples in the human hippocampus during sleep" — Nature Neuroscience**
  - Demonstrated the temporally organized relationship between major NREM sleep rhythms implicated in memory consolidation.
  - Important for understanding why sleep stage alone may not fully describe periods of optimal memory processing.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4119

- **Cairney et al. (2018) — "Memory Consolidation Is Linked to Spindle-Mediated Information Processing during Sleep" — Current Biology**
  - Linked spindle activity to processing of memory-related information during sleep.
  - Relevant to whether cue delivery should depend on physiological events within NREM rather than stage classification alone.

- **Schreiner et al. (2021) — "Endogenous Memory Reactivation during Sleep in Humans Is Clocked by Slow Oscillation-Spindle Complexes" — Nature Communications**
  - Showed that endogenous memory reactivation is temporally related to coordinated slow-oscillation and spindle activity.
  - Directly relevant to the biological timing assumptions underlying closed-loop TMR.

- **Ngo & Staresina (2022) — "Shaping Overnight Consolidation via Slow-Oscillation Closed-Loop Targeted Memory Reactivation" — PNAS**
  - Directly compared TMR cues delivered at different phases of ongoing slow oscillations.
  - UP-state cueing reduced overnight forgetting relative to DOWN-state cueing and was associated with stronger spindle responses.
  - This is one of the most important papers for challenging the Phase-I choice to begin with stage-aware rather than phase-aware control.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2123428119

### Recent Work

- **Staresina et al. (2023) — "How Coupled Slow Oscillations, Spindles and Ripples Coordinate Neuronal Processing and Communication during Human Sleep" — Nature Neuroscience**
  - Used human intracranial recordings to investigate how slow oscillations, spindles, and ripples coordinate neuronal activity during sleep.
  - Reinforces the importance of sleep microstructure rather than treating NREM as a uniform physiological state.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01381-w

- **Schreiner et al. (2023) — "Respiration Modulates Sleep Oscillations and Memory Reactivation in Humans" — Nature Communications**
  - Found that respiration is temporally linked with sleep oscillations and memory reactivation.
  - Relevant because it shows that memory-related sleep physiology may be influenced by additional rhythmic processes beyond conventional stage labels.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-43450-5

- **Staresina (2024) — "Coupled Sleep Rhythms for Memory Consolidation" — Trends in Cognitive Sciences**
  - Reviews evidence that coordinated slow oscillations, spindles, and hippocampal ripples form an important temporal framework for sleep-dependent memory consolidation.
  - Particularly relevant for evaluating whether event-level physiology should influence future TMR control architectures.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2024.02.002

- **Thom et al. (2025) — "Human Sleep Spindles Track Experimentally Excited Brain Networks"**
  - Recent work involving Staresina examining the relationship between waking neural activation and subsequent sleep-spindle expression.
  - Relevant to the idea that sleep oscillations reflect memory-specific neural processing rather than only generic stage physiology.

## Relevance to Neuro-TMR

Staresina is especially valuable for evaluating the boundary between **stage-aware** and **event/phase-aware** TMR.

Phase I currently treats stage-aware control as the more defensible first-generation pathway, while precise slow-wave phase or event-level targeting is considered a possible later refinement.

His work directly tests whether that simplification is reasonable.

He can help determine whether:

- broad NREM/N3 detection provides sufficient physiological information for an initial system;
- slow-oscillation phase materially changes TMR outcomes;
- spindle timing should influence cue scheduling;
- event-level EEG observability should be considered an essential system requirement rather than an optional optimization.

He is also highly relevant to the EEG-first decision because the physiological events studied by his group are directly observable through electrophysiology rather than peripheral sleep-stage inference alone.

## Phase-I Claims They Can Challenge

- **Stage-aware TMR is a defensible first-generation simplification.**
  - His closed-loop phase-specific TMR work can help determine whether stage-level targeting is sufficient or whether slow-oscillation timing is already scientifically important enough to require earlier implementation.

- **NREM/N3 detection is the principal physiological requirement for cue scheduling.**
  - His work suggests that physiological conditions within a sleep stage are heterogeneous and that slow oscillations, spindles, and ripples may define more informative intervention windows.

- **Phase-aware targeting can reasonably be deferred until a later generation.**
  - He is well positioned to assess whether this sequencing is justified by behavioral evidence or merely convenient from an engineering perspective.

- **Reduced wearable EEG may provide sufficient physiological observability for closed-loop TMR.**
  - His research can inform which electrophysiological events actually need to be measurable, even if he is not primarily a wearable-device validation researcher.
