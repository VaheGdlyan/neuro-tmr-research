## Why This Expert Matters

Cecilia Forcato is particularly relevant because she combines direct human TMR research with current work on sleep, memory, EEG, neuromodulation, and applied neuroscience.

Her work is especially valuable for challenging one of the most important Phase-I decisions: whether a first-generation TMR system should strongly prioritize N3/SWS or whether broader NREM targeting may be scientifically sufficient.

Her laboratory also explicitly combines basic and applied neuroscience and studies brain activity in real time while exploring non-pharmacological interventions. This gives her relevance not only to the behavioral TMR evidence but also to the transition toward automated intervention.

## Relevant Research

### Foundational Work

- **Forcato et al. (2020) — "Reactivation during sleep with incomplete reminder cues rather than complete ones stabilizes long-term memory in humans" — Communications Biology**
  - Investigated how different reminder structures influence memory stabilization during sleep.
  - Showed that memory reactivation during sleep is more complex than simply replaying a complete learning cue and automatically strengthening a memory.
  - Particularly relevant to cue design and the relationship between reactivation, stabilization, and reconsolidation.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-020-01457-4

- **Carbone et al. (2021) — "The effect of zolpidem on targeted memory reactivation during sleep" — Learning & Memory**
  - Investigated TMR together with pharmacological manipulation of sleep physiology.
  - Relevant because it connects memory-reactivation outcomes with the underlying physiological characteristics of sleep.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.052787.120

### Recent Work

- **Carbone et al. (2024) — "Comparing targeted memory reactivation during slow wave sleep and sleep stage 2" — Scientific Reports**
  - Directly compared learning-associated cue presentation during SWS and stage N2.
  - Contrary to the study hypothesis, memory performance was not significantly better when cues were delivered during SWS.
  - Electrophysiological responses differed between stages, but these differences did not translate into a superior behavioral outcome for SWS.
  - Highly relevant to whether Neuro-TMR should strongly prioritize N3 rather than broader NREM targeting.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-59696-y

- **Vidal et al. (2025) — "Short naps improve subsequent learning in a high school setting" — npj Science of Learning**
  - Extends the laboratory's sleep-memory research toward applied educational settings.
  - Not a direct TMR validation study, but useful evidence of the group's continuing translational interest in sleep and learning.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00307-4

## Relevance to Neuro-TMR

Forcato can challenge several assumptions that sit directly between TMR neuroscience and the proposed technical pathway.

Most importantly, her work provides a direct empirical challenge to treating N3/SWS as necessarily the optimal behavioral window for TMR.

The 2024 SWS-vs-N2 study suggests an important distinction:

> stronger or more characteristic N3 physiology does not automatically imply a larger TMR-related behavioral benefit.

Her work on incomplete reminder cues is also relevant because it shows that cue structure and the state of the memory itself influence what happens following reactivation.

Finally, her current laboratory's interest in real-time brain activity and non-invasive intervention makes her particularly relevant for discussing which physiological variables an automated TMR system should actually monitor.

## Phase-I Claims They Can Challenge

- **N3/SWS should be the preferred first-generation TMR stage.**
  - The 2024 direct comparison found no significant behavioral advantage for SWS over N2 in that paradigm.

- **Sleep-stage classification provides the main information needed to determine cue eligibility.**
  - Her current real-time and electrophysiological work makes her well positioned to assess whether additional features of brain state should influence intervention timing.

- **TMR can be represented primarily as reactivation followed by strengthening.**
  - Her work on complete versus incomplete reminders shows that reminder structure, mismatch, stabilization, and memory state can alter the consequences of reactivation.

- **Cue scheduling can be designed largely independently of the characteristics of the memory being targeted.**
  - Her memory-reactivation research can help assess whether memory state and reminder construction need to be treated as explicit system variables.
