## Why This Expert Matters

Delphine Oudiette is particularly valuable because she connects two parts of the Neuro-TMR problem that are often treated separately.

First, she helped establish the conceptual framework of Targeted Memory Reactivation through foundational work with Ken Paller on manipulating memory reactivation during sleep.

Second, her current research focuses on the fact that sleep is not a uniform state. Her DreamTeam investigates hybrid states between wakefulness and sleep and transient periods in which sleeping people can continue to process and even behaviorally respond to external information.

This makes her unusually well positioned to challenge a central assumption in an automated TMR system:

> Is knowing the conventional sleep stage enough to know whether the brain is currently receptive to an external memory cue?

Her work suggests that moment-to-moment responsiveness may vary substantially even within states conventionally classified as sleep.

## Relevant Research

### Foundational Work

- **Oudiette & Paller (2013) — "Upgrading the Sleeping Brain with Targeted Memory Reactivation" — Trends in Cognitive Sciences**
  - One of the foundational conceptual papers defining TMR and its use for experimentally biasing memory consolidation during sleep.
  - Discussed both the potential and the limitations of manipulating memory processing using learning-associated sensory cues.
  - Directly relevant to the conceptual foundation of Neuro-TMR.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2013.01.006

- **Oudiette, Antony, Creery & Paller (2013) — "The Role of Memory Reactivation During Wakefulness and Sleep in Determining Which Memories Endure" — Journal of Neuroscience**
  - Investigated how reactivation influences which recently learned memories persist.
  - Relevant to memory selection and the idea that reactivation does not affect all memories uniformly.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.5497-12.2013

- **Creery, Oudiette, Antony & Paller (2015) — "Targeted Memory Reactivation during Sleep Depends on Prior Learning" — Sleep**
  - Demonstrated that TMR benefits depend on the quality of prior learning.
  - Important because a practical TMR system cannot treat cue presentation independently of the state of the memory established before sleep.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.5665/sleep.4670

### Recent Work

- **Türker et al. (2023) — "Behavioral and brain responses to verbal stimuli reveal transient periods of cognitive integration of the external world during sleep" — Nature Neuroscience**
  - Showed that sleepers can enter transient periods in which they process verbal information and produce behavioral responses while remaining asleep.
  - Responsiveness varied dynamically rather than mapping perfectly onto a simple awake-versus-asleep distinction.
  - Highly relevant to determining whether sleep stage alone is sufficient for deciding when external TMR cues should be delivered.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01449-7

- **Lacaux, Strauss, Bekinschtein & Oudiette (2024) — "Embracing Sleep-Onset Complexity" — Trends in Neurosciences**
  - Argues for treating sleep onset as a multidimensional and dynamically changing state rather than a simple discrete transition.
  - Relevant to the wider problem of relying exclusively on conventional categorical sleep labels for intervention control.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tins.2024.02.002

- **Wong et al. (2025) — "A Dream EEG and Mentation Database" — Nature Communications**
  - Introduced the DREAM database, combining standardized sleep M/EEG with reports of subjective sleep experiences across a large multi-laboratory dataset.
  - Demonstrated that objective EEG features can contain information about ongoing conscious experience even within the same broad sleep categories.
  - Relevant to the idea that conventional stage classification may not capture all physiologically meaningful aspects of the sleeping brain.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-61945-1

### Current Research Focus

Oudiette currently co-leads the DreamTeam at Paris Brain Institute.

Her research focuses on:

- sleep and cognition;
- hybrid states between sleep and wakefulness;
- sensory processing during sleep;
- moment-to-moment responsiveness to external stimuli;
- consciousness and dreaming;
- memory;
- high-density EEG, EOG and EMG;
- behavioral responses during sleep;
- multidimensional characterization of sleep state.

The broader goal is to understand sleep as a dynamic physiological and cognitive process rather than only as a sequence of discrete conventional stages.

## Relevance to Neuro-TMR

Oudiette is uniquely relevant because she can challenge the assumption that:

> correct sleep-stage classification = correct cueing opportunity.

Phase I currently proposes stage-aware closed-loop TMR as a defensible first-generation simplification.

Her recent work raises a deeper question:

> Within a nominally appropriate sleep stage, are there transient periods when the sleeping brain is more or less capable of integrating external information?

If so, future cue eligibility may eventually need to incorporate markers of:

- sensory responsiveness;
- arousal proximity;
- local or hybrid sleep/wake features;
- ongoing cognitive state;

rather than relying only on the conventional N1/N2/N3/REM label.

She can also evaluate whether our description of TMR oversimplifies the relationship between:

> stimulus presentation → sensory processing → memory reactivation.

These are not necessarily the same event.

## Phase-I Claims They Can Challenge

- **Sleep stage is the principal state variable required to determine when a TMR cue should be delivered.**
  - Her recent work demonstrates considerable moment-to-moment variability in responsiveness to external information during sleep.

- **Stage-aware control is an adequate first-generation representation of cue eligibility.**
  - She can help determine whether this is a reasonable engineering simplification or whether additional responsiveness/arousal markers are scientifically important from the beginning.

- **Presenting an associated cue during an appropriate sleep state can be treated as evidence that the sleeping brain processed it in the intended manner.**
  - Her work directly separates stimulus presentation from actual cognitive integration of external information.

- **Conventional categorical sleep architecture sufficiently represents the physiological state relevant to intervention.**
  - Her current research emphasizes hybrid and multidimensional sleep states that may cross conventional stage boundaries.

- **TMR effectiveness can be considered largely independently of prior learning strength.**
  - Her earlier TMR work showed that benefits depend on the strength of prior learning.
