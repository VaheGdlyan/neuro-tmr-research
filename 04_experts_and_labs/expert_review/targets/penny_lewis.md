## Why This Expert Matters

Penny Lewis is especially valuable because her laboratory studies TMR across multiple sleep stages, memory domains, and intervention strategies.

Her work is useful for challenging an overly narrow model in which TMR is primarily an N3-based declarative-memory intervention.

Her group also works on neural decoding, closed-loop auditory stimulation, and efficient online sleep-stage classification, making her relevant to both the neuroscience and technical translation of Neuro-TMR.

## Relevant Research

### Foundational Work
- **Cousins et al. (2014) — "Cued Memory Reactivation During Slow-Wave Sleep Promotes Explicit Knowledge of a Motor Sequence"**
  - Demonstrated TMR effects on procedural/motor learning.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1011-14.2014

- **Cairney et al. (2014) — "Targeted Memory Reactivation During Slow Wave Sleep Facilitates Emotional Memory Consolidation"**
  - Important early evidence linking TMR, emotional memories, and sleep spindles.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.5665/sleep.3572

### Recent Work
- **Rakowska et al. (2024) — "Cueing memory reactivation during NREM sleep engenders long-term plasticity in both brain and behaviour"**
  - Relevant to the persistence of neural and behavioral consequences of TMR.

- **Greco et al. (2025) — "Disarming emotional memories using Targeted Memory Reactivation during Rapid Eye Movement sleep"**
  - Important because it extends TMR intervention beyond NREM and toward emotional regulation.

- **Abdellahi et al. (2026) — "Targeted memory reactivation elicits temporally compressed reactivation linked to spindles"**
  - Relevant to identifying neural signatures of actual reactivation rather than relying only on sleep-stage detection.

## Relevance to Neuro-TMR

Lewis can challenge whether our first-generation pathway is too narrowly centered on N3/SWS.

Her work also raises the distinction between:
- detecting the correct sleep state;
- triggering a cue;
- and demonstrating that memory reactivation actually occurred.

## Phase-I Claims They Can Challenge
- N3/NREM should be the primary intervention stage for first-generation TMR.
- Stage detection is sufficient to define cue eligibility.
- Memory enhancement is the main relevant TMR outcome.
- Neural reactivation does not need to be measured directly in an early system.
