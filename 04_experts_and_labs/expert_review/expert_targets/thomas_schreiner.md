## Why This Expert Matters

Thomas Schreiner is highly relevant because his work connects behavioral TMR with the electrophysiological mechanisms of memory reactivation.

He has worked on vocabulary TMR, slow oscillation-spindle coupling, endogenous memory reactivation, and more recently respiration as a potential organizer of sleep-memory physiology.

He can therefore challenge both the stage-aware model and our assumption that EEG alone contains all relevant intervention information.

## Relevant Research

### Foundational Work
- **Schreiner & Rasch (2015) — "Boosting Vocabulary Learning by Verbal Cueing During Sleep"**
  - Strongly relevant to Neuro-TMR's interest in structured learning and vocabulary.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhu139

- **Schreiner et al. (2021) — "Endogenous memory reactivation during sleep in humans is clocked by slow oscillation-spindle complexes"**
  - Shows that endogenous memory reactivation is temporally associated with coordinated sleep oscillations.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-23520-2

### Recent Work
- **Schreiner et al. (2023) — "Respiration modulates sleep oscillations and memory reactivation in humans"**
  - Suggests that respiration is linked to the timing of sleep oscillations and memory reactivation.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-43450-5

- **Schwimmbeck et al. (2025) — "A role for respiration in coordinating sleep oscillations and memory consolidation"**
  - Extends the brain-body interaction perspective on sleep-memory processing.
  - DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tins.2025.02.005

## Relevance to Neuro-TMR

Schreiner can evaluate whether:
- sleep stage is enough to identify useful cueing windows;
- SO-spindle complexes should be incorporated earlier;
- physiological variables outside EEG may provide useful information;
- vocabulary learning is an appropriate initial behavioral application.

## Phase-I Claims They Can Challenge
- Stage-aware targeting is sufficient for V1.
- EEG sleep-stage detection is the main physiological sensing problem.
- Vocabulary learning is a straightforward first TMR application.
- Brain-body signals are secondary rather than potentially informative for intervention timing.
